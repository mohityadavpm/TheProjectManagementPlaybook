What this is

A practitioner signal, not a population estimate

This research set out to test a simple question: do projects fail from a lack of frameworks, or from a gap between knowing the discipline and applying it consistently? Twenty-seven polls from a 30-part LinkedIn series, run over a year, gathered practitioner views across initiation, planning, execution, monitoring, and closure.

The recurring pattern across all 27 polls: practitioners are less often missing a tool or technique, and more often missing consistent follow-through on objective clarity, ownership, and closure.

Findings are self-reported, platform-based, and directional — not a statistically representative or causal sample. Poll sizes range from 20 to 505 votes, and 10 of the 27 had fewer than 100. Only each poll’s top answer share was recorded, so percentages are never pooled. Full methodology and limitations are in the report.

Delivery models in practice

Waterfall, Agile, and the hybrid middle ground

The poll set doesn’t directly measure which model respondents use — but related findings show where structure and adaptability each show up in practice.

Waterfall & predictive

76%favour RACI, 41%a formal charter, 69%EVM for budget tracking — strong reliance on upfront structure and control.

Agile & adaptive

51%pick Agile as the framework for continuous improvement, and 64%see stakeholder engagement as the top lever against resistance to change.

Hybrid

Governance habits and Agile habits coexist in the same respondents. The data doesn’t show hybrid outperforming — only that it should be designed deliberately, not assumed.

Frameworks provide structure; outcomes depend on discipline. No methodology in this dataset is shown to consistently outperform another.

Leader brief

Five takeaways for leaders

Short on time? These are the five moves the poll evidence suggests leaders act on first.

01

Start with clarity, not activity

79% name defining objectives as the first planning step (Poll #18). Leader move: do not approve a project without clear, measurable objectives.

02

Value is often lost at closure

Stakeholder disengagement is the most commonly cited closure barrier at 34% (Poll #30). Leader move: plan handover, adoption and ownership from day one, not at the end.

03

Ownership drives improvement

Lack of ownership is the top barrier to continuous improvement at 35% (Poll #29). Leader move: name an accountable owner for every outcome and every lesson learned.

04

Tools do not replace discipline

61% track progress with PM tools (Poll #24), yet core challenges persist. Leader move: track adoption and outcomes, not just milestones and dashboards.

05

Capacity is the hidden constraint

Uneven workload distribution leads resource challenges at 43% (Poll #25). Leader move: review workload balance before adding scope or new projects.

From insight to action

A 90-day leader plan

Each action traces back to a poll result or pitfall in the report. No new statistics are introduced.

Days 1–30

Diagnose & clarify

  • Confirm every live project has written, agreed objectives
  • Map stakeholders and test their commitment
  • Review team availability and workload spread
  • Check every risk has a named owner
Days 31–60

Assign ownership & embed governance

  • Require a signed charter and a RACI
  • Set priorities with MoSCoW and record trade-offs
  • Rebalance workload and share a current view of capacity
  • Engage stakeholders to reduce resistance to change
Days 61–90

Close the loop & improve

  • Track progress in tools, but review decisions and outcomes
  • Name an owner for improvement and adoption after handover
  • Make closure a governance step: acceptance, knowledge transfer, lessons learned
  • Measure adoption and value alongside time, cost, and scope
By day 90, every project should have clear objectives, named owners, visible capacity, and a closure path that hands over to sustained ownership. The full report adds a 15-question leader checklist with scoring.

What it means for each role

Three audiences, three sets of actions

PMOs & governance

Treat objective definition and closure as governance gates, not paperwork — and shift portfolio oversight toward outcome realisation, not just activity tracking.

Project managers

Anchor planning in clearly stated objectives, manage stakeholder engagement proactively, and use tools to support decisions rather than replace judgment.

Business leaders

Define success beyond time and cost, fund change management explicitly, and assign clear post-delivery ownership so value is actually realised.
